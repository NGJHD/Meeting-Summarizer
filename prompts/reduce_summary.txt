Organise the body BY THEME, not chronologically. This matters more than any other
instruction here. A chronological retelling of a meeting this long is nearly as long as
the transcript and nearly as useless: it reproduces the meeting's own disorder, where
one topic is raised, dropped, and returned to twice. Gather everything said about a
topic into one place, however scattered it was in time, and give the reader the
resolved state of that topic.

Where a theme was discussed at several points, cite the timestamps together.

Return exactly this structure as Markdown, with no preamble and no closing commentary:

# Summary — {{MEETING_NAME}}
*Meeting Duration: {{DURATION}}*

## Executive Summary
Three to five sentences of flowing prose. No bullets. What the meeting was for, what it
settled, and what it did not.

## Key Themes
The substance of the meeting, organised by topic. Flowing prose under short topic
sub-headings (###). Not bullets. Give each theme the space its importance warrants
rather than an equal share.

A heading may join closely related aspects of one subject: "Budget Review & Forecasting" is
one theme. It may not join separate systems, products or deliverables that merely happen
to be the same kind of thing — "Third-Party Systems: Payroll, CRM & Ticketing Portal"
is three themes and must be split into three, and "Budget Review, Forecasting & Office
Move" is two. The test is whether a reader looking for one of the things named in
the heading would find everything the meeting said about it underneath. Splitting like
this does not mean writing less about each; do not let a theme shrink to a single line.

This section carries the whole meeting. There is no separate section for decisions,
disagreements or open questions, so each belongs inside the theme it arose in: say what
was settled and when (HH:MM:SS), where participants differed and whether it resolved,
and what was left unanswered. Do not promote discussion to decision, and do not add
section headings beyond the ### topic ones.
